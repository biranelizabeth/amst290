The Met

The world's most watched fashion event, and the institutional machinery that translates subcultural aesthetics into mass spectacle.

Background

The Event and Its Machinery

The Met Gala — formally the Costume Institute Benefit — is the Metropolitan Museum of Art's annual fundraising gala for its Costume Institute. Held each May on the first Monday, it is widely regarded as the most culturally powerful fashion event in the world, not merely because of who attends, but because of what it broadcasts.

Founded in 1948 by fashion publicist Eleanor Lambert, the gala was for decades a relatively low-profile industry gathering. Its transformation into a global cultural spectacle accelerated sharply under the leadership of Vogue editor-in-chief Anna Wintour, who took over as chair in 1995. Under Wintour, the guest list became curated to include a precise mixture of fashion, film, music, sports, and technology celebrity — a formula engineered for maximum media impact.

Crucially, each gala is themed to the Costume Institute's annual exhibition. The theme sets a dress code that celebrities, designers, and brands must interpret on the red carpet. McLean (2023) characterizes the Met Gala as precisely this kind of site: one where high fashion, popular culture, and mass media intersect and collide, producing a cultural statement that none of these registers could generate alone. The theme is the mechanism through which that collision is organized — and broadcast simultaneously to a global audience.

The Met Gala is not just a party. It is a curated act of cultural production — an institution deciding, for one night a year, what ideas are worth wearing.

Why the Theme Matters

The gala's theme is not merely decorative. Because attendees must interpret and embody the theme on one of the most photographed red carpets in the world, the theme functions as a public brief — compelling fashion's most powerful figures to engage with its conceptual content in front of a global audience of hundreds of millions.

Past themes have explored religion (Heavenly Bodies, 2018), technology (Manus x Machina, 2016), and Japanese fashion (Japan: Art, Craft, Design, 2024). When the theme is Camp, the stakes for subcultural meaning are uniquely high.

2019

Camp:
Notes on Fashion

The 2019 Met Gala, held on May 6, 2019, was themed "Camp: Notes on Fashion" in direct homage to Susan Sontag's landmark 1964 essay "Notes on Camp." The accompanying exhibition ran from May 9 to September 8, 2019, at the Metropolitan Museum's Costume Institute.

The exhibition presented approximately 250 objects spanning fashion from the seventeenth century to the present, exploring how camp — defined broadly as artifice, humor, exaggeration, and the love of the unnatural — has manifested in fashion across centuries. Iconic costume designer Bob Mackie and legendary couturier Charles James both featured prominently alongside contemporary designers like Moschino, Versace, and Gucci.

The gala was co-chaired by Lady Gaga, Harry Styles, Serena Williams, and Alessandro Michele (then creative director of Gucci). The co-chairs themselves were carefully chosen: Lady Gaga as a pop maximalist deeply invested in camp aesthetics; Harry Styles as a boundary-crossing style icon; Serena Williams as one of the most powerful athletes in the world; and Michele as the architect of Gucci's own neo-camp aesthetic revival.

The Costume Institute Exhibition

Camp: Notes on Fashion ran May 9 – Sept 8, 2019. The exhibition presented ~250 objects including haute couture, ready-to-wear, and accessories, exploring camp as a "sensibility" (in Sontag's terminology) across fashion history.

Organized around Sontag's 58 "notes," the exhibition traced camp's intersection with queerness, artifice, humor, and fashion's relationship to the unnatural and the theatrical.

Data

Media Impact by the Numbers

Launchmetrics, a fashion analytics firm, published a detailed analysis of the 2019 Met Gala's Media Impact Value (MIV®) — a proprietary metric that assigns monetary value to media placements across print, online, and social channels, accounting for platform, placement tier, and reach.

Their findings illuminate which celebrities generated the most cultural currency, which brands benefited most, and how the event's media footprint was distributed across platforms. The data provides quantitative evidence for what the red carpet's visual spectacle already suggested: that the 2019 Met Gala was not just an event — it was a media mechanism for producing and distributing aesthetic meaning at mass scale.

MIV® Defined

Media Impact Value (MIV®) is Launchmetrics' proprietary benchmark for measuring the impact of all media placements and mentions across the fashion, luxury, and beauty industries. It translates reach and engagement across print, online, and social media into a single comparable monetary figure.

Social Media

The Event as Social Media Engine

The 2019 Met Gala was the first edition to be live-streamed in full by Vogue, and the first in which social media surpassed print as the primary generator of MIV. This structural shift marks a qualitative change in how the event functions culturally.

Previous galas were primarily documented after the fact — through editorial spreads in Vogue, fashion week coverage, and retrospective analysis. In 2019, the gala was experienced by millions in real time, with #MetGala2019 trending globally on Twitter within the first hour of arrivals. Instagram Stories, Twitter live-threads, and YouTube reaction content produced a distributed, participatory media event that extended far beyond the event's physical boundaries.

This simultaneity is crucial: camp aesthetics were not merely reported on — they were experienced in real time by a mass audience, many of whom encountered the concept of camp for the first time through a celebrity's look on a phone screen. The translation from subcultural practice to mass aesthetic began at the moment of broadcast.
